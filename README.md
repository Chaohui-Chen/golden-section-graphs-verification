# Computational verification

**Paper:** *A complete characterization of graphs with second largest eigenvalue at most $(\sqrt{5}-1)/2$*

**Authors:** Chaohui Chen, Hangxi Cha, Haiying Shan, and Muhuo Liu.

This repository contains verification code and reference outputs supporting the finite computations in the accompanying paper. Result and section numbers used below refer to that paper.

The verification materials consist of the following files:

```text
.
├── README.md
├── verify.py
└── expected_output.txt
```

| File | Purpose | Dependencies or origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [README.md](README.md) | Instructions and correspondence with the paper. | Documentation. |
| [verify.py](verify.py) | Script reproducing the finite assertions invoked in Lemmas 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and Proposition 3.6. | Python and NetworkX. |
| [expected_output.txt](expected_output.txt) | Captured reference output of the verification script. | Generated by `python verify.py`; not used as input. |

The script `verify.py` reproduces the finite assertions cited in the paper: the seven exceptional graphs in Lemma 2.5, the enumeration in Lemma 3.1, the forbidden induced subgraphs in Lemma 3.2, and the classification in Proposition 3.6. NetworkX supplies the Graph Atlas used as input. The script requires only Python and NetworkX.

## 1. Requirements and execution

python -m pip install "networkx>=3,<4"

Exact field elements in the output are printed as $a+b\sqrt{5}$ with rational $a,b$. These are equivalent to the expressions in $\sigma$ used in the paper, since $\sqrt{5}=1+2\sigma$. For example,

$$
\frac{5}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}=2-\sigma,\qquad
9+3\sqrt{5}=12+6\sigma.
$$

## 2. Notation and terminology

Throughout,

$$
\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},\qquad
\varphi=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}=1+\sigma,\qquad
M_F=A(F)+\varphi I.
$$

Here $J$ is the all-ones matrix, and $n_+(M)$, $n_0(M)$, and $n_-(M)$ denote the numbers of positive, zero, and negative eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix $M$, respectively, counted with multiplicity.

The family $\mathcal H_+$ is explicitly defined in Section 2: it consists of the graphs $W_{t,j}$ and the seven exceptional graphs. Proposition 3.4 identifies it with the connected graphs satisfying $\lambda_{\min}\ge-\varphi$.

The family $\mathcal F$ in Section 3 consists of minimal forbidden induced subgraphs for the bound $\lambda_{\min}\ge-\varphi$ whose complements additionally satisfy $\lambda_2\le\sigma$. Proposition 3.6 gives

$$
\mathcal F=\{S_1,\ldots,S_5\}.
$$

These are not all minimal forbidden induced subgraphs for the least-eigenvalue bound without the extra complement condition.

The graphs $F_1,\ldots,F_4$ are the separate fixed forbidden induced subgraphs defined in Section 3. The function `forbidden_graphs()` constructs those four graphs; `minimal_forbidden_graphs()` constructs $S_1,\ldots,S_5$.

## 3. Correspondence with the paper

The `main()` function in `verify.py` invokes the following checks in the order of the corresponding results in the paper.

### Lemma 2.5 — `check_positive_exceptions()`

For each exceptional graph $F\in\mathcal H_+$, check $M_F\succeq0$, solve

$$
M_Fx=\mathbf{1}
$$

to establish the range condition, and verify

$$
\eta(F)=\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}x
$$

against the stated shifted-coronal value. All computations are exact. The formulas for $W_{t,j}$ with arbitrary parameters are proved analytically in the paper, not established by this finite calculation. Thus the output heading “Lemma 2.5 / seven exceptional graphs in H_plus” refers specifically to this finite part of the lemma.

### Lemma 3.1 — `check_positive_classification(atlas)`

Examine every nonempty connected Graph Atlas graph through order seven. Check that $M_F\succeq0$ holds exactly for graphs isomorphic to an explicitly constructed member of $\mathcal H_+$ in this order range.

The seven exceptional graphs have order at most six, so the four passing graphs of order seven all belong to $\mathcal W$.

### Lemma 3.2 — `check_fixed_forbidden()`

- **Part (i):** check $\lambda_{\min}(F)<-\varphi$ for $F\in\{S_1,\ldots,S_5,C_4,F_1\}$.
- **Part (ii):** check $\lambda_2(\overline F)>\sigma$ for $C_4,F_1,\ldots,F_4$, all 25 ordered constructions $S_i\mathbin{\dot\cup}S_j$, and the five graphs

$$
\mathrm{Sp}^{*}(S_i,1)
=(K_1\vee S_i)\mathbin{\dot\cup}K_1,
\qquad 1\le i\le5.
$$

The 25 ordered pairs are not claimed to be pairwise nonisomorphic.

### Proposition 3.6 — `check_minimal_forbidden_classification(atlas)`

Retain exactly the graphs $S$ satisfying all three conditions:

$$
n_-(M_S)\ge1,\qquad
n_-(M_{S-v})=0\quad\text{for every }v\in V(S),\qquad
n_-(M_S-J)\le1.
$$

Check that the retained graphs are exactly $S_1,\ldots,S_5$, each once up to isomorphism. Check the defining conditions directly for each $S_i$ as well.

Lemma 3.5, proved in the paper, bounds the order of every member of $\mathcal F$ by seven and makes this enumeration sufficient for Proposition 3.6.

## 4. Exact arithmetic and input

All spectral tests use rational arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})$, represented as $a+b\sqrt{5}$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Q$. Signs are determined by rational comparisons; when $a$ and $b$ have opposite signs, compare $a^2$ with $5b^2$.

The routine `inertia()` uses symmetric congruence elimination. A nonzero diagonal entry supplies a $1\times1$ pivot. If all diagonal entries are zero but an off-diagonal entry is nonzero, a $2\times2$ principal block with inertia $(1,0,1)$ supplies the pivot. A zero remaining matrix contributes its order to the nullity. Singular matrices require no numerical tolerance.

For a nonempty graph $F$,

$$
\lambda_{\min}(F)\ge-\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n_-(M_F)=0.
$$

For a graph $F$ of order at least two,

$$
\lambda_2(\overline F)\le\sigma
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n_-(M_F-J)\le1.
$$

The second test follows from

$$
M_F-J=\sigma I-A(\overline F)
$$

and does not require the complement to be connected. The range condition is tested by solving $M_Fx=\mathbf{1}$ exactly, rather than using a numerical pseudoinverse.

NetworkX `graph_atlas_g()`, based on Read and Wilson's *An Atlas of Graphs* (1998), supplies one representative of each unlabeled graph of order at most seven. The program selects the nonempty connected graphs and checks their counts before applying the spectral tests:

| Order | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Connected graphs | 1 | 1 | 2 | 6 | 21 | 112 | 853 |
| $\lambda_{\min}\ge-\varphi$ | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 7 | 4 |

The search is exhaustive over these 996 connected graphs. The constructors encode the graph definitions in the paper, and isomorphism tests identify the passing graphs. The enumeration relies on the completeness of the Graph Atlas; the per-order counts provide an integrity check on the input.

## 5. Scope of verification

The programmatic verification covers the finite calculations described in Section 3 of this README. The extension lemma, the arbitrary-order $\mathcal H_+$ classification, the order bound for $\mathcal F$, the structural decompositions in Sections 4 and 5, and the general coronal formulas in Section 6 are established by the proofs in the paper.
